# Feature Specification: Early Media Relay for Outbound Calls

**Feature Branch**: `037-p-early-media`
**Created**: 2026-08-15
**Status**: Draft
**Input**: User description: "Relay P-Early-Media (RFC 5009) audio for outbound calls: when the carrier sends a provisional response (180/181/182/183) carrying SDP before the final 200 OK — e.g. Jio's ~13.7s "P-Early-Media: sendonly" announcement on outbound attempts — the bridge should relay that early media audio to the local caller (PBX/softphone leg on Agent B) instead of discarding it, so the caller hears the network's announcement/ringback instead of silence during the ringing window. Mirrors the existing inbound pattern where Endpoint::pair_calls conference-bridges two PJSIP call legs before either is answered. Scope: outbound calls only. Must handle teardown correctly if the caller cancels or the carrier fails while early media is active."

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-08-15

- Q: FR-003 requires handoff from pre-answer audio to answered-call audio with "no audible interruption noticeable to the caller," but doesn't specify how much of a gap (if any) is tolerable. Should this be zero-gap (same audio path carries through unchanged) or is a brief sub-second reset acceptable? → A: Zero gap — the same audio path must carry through from pre-answer into the answered call, with no perceptible break at all.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Caller hears the carrier's pre-answer announcement (Priority: P1)

A person places an outbound call through the bridge (dialing out from a PBX
extension or softphone toward a real phone number on the GSM/VoWiFi
network). Before the far end actually answers, the carrier's network plays
an announcement or tone — for example, an operator message about the
number dialed, or ringback generated by the network rather than the local
device. Today the caller hears nothing at all during this window; with this
feature, the caller hears whatever the carrier is actually sending, the
moment it starts.

**Why this priority**: This is the core of the feature and the reason it
exists — a real outbound-calling failure was hard to diagnose specifically
*because* the caller heard silence instead of an audible carrier message
that would have explained what was happening. Without this story there is
no feature.

**Independent Test**: Place an outbound call through a carrier known to
send a pre-answer announcement (observed on Jio) and confirm the caller
hears it, timed against when the carrier actually sent it.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an outbound call has been placed and the carrier sends a
   provisional response carrying playable audio before answering, **When**
   that audio arrives, **Then** the caller hears it immediately, without
   waiting for the carrier to formally answer the call.
2. **Given** the caller is hearing the carrier's pre-answer audio, **When**
   the carrier answers the call for real, **Then** the caller's audio
   continues without an audible gap, restart, or double ring/tone.
3. **Given** a carrier that never sends any pre-answer audio (today's
   behavior), **When** an outbound call is placed to it, **Then** the
   caller experiences call setup exactly as before (no regression).

---

### User Story 2 - Abandoning a call during the announcement leaves nothing stuck (Priority: P2)

A caller who is listening to the carrier's pre-answer announcement decides
not to continue and hangs up before the call is answered. Both the local
leg and the carrier leg must end cleanly — nothing should be left
half-connected because early audio had already started flowing.

**Why this priority**: Early media relay introduces a new in-between state
(audio flowing on a call that isn't formally answered yet) that didn't
exist before. If teardown from that state isn't handled, this feature
trades a diagnosability problem for a reliability one, which is worse.

**Independent Test**: Place an outbound call to a carrier that sends a
pre-answer announcement, hang up the local leg partway through the
announcement, and confirm both legs end and no call is left active or
orphaned.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the caller is hearing the carrier's pre-answer audio, **When**
   the caller hangs up before the carrier answers, **Then** the
   in-progress carrier call is abandoned and both legs are reported as
   ended.
2. **Given** the caller is hearing the carrier's pre-answer audio, **When**
   the carrier instead fails or rejects the call before answering, **Then**
   the caller's leg is ended cleanly rather than left open with no further
   audio.

---

### User Story 3 - Carrier-side setup problems are audible on the first attempt (Priority: P3)

An operator troubleshooting an outbound-calling problem can now tell, by
listening to a single test call, whether the carrier is sending an
announcement (and what it says) instead of having to infer it from logs or
packet captures after the fact.

**Why this priority**: This is the diagnostic payoff of Story 1, called out
separately because it's a distinct value even though it rides on the same
mechanism — it changes how a *future* problem gets investigated, not just
this one.

**Independent Test**: Reproduce the originally diagnosed scenario (an
outbound attempt to a carrier that plays an announcement instead of
completing the call) and confirm the announcement is audible without
inspecting logs or captures.

---

### Edge Cases

- The carrier retransmits the same pre-answer response (a duplicate,
  network-level resend rather than a new update) — the caller must not
  hear the announcement restart or double up.
- The carrier sends more than one *distinct* pre-answer update (e.g. an
  announcement followed by ringback) before finally answering — later
  audio replaces earlier audio without a jarring gap.
- The pre-answer audio is followed directly by call failure (busy, not
  reachable, network reject) rather than by an answer — the caller hears
  the announcement, then the call ends, rather than hanging in an
  unresolved state.
- The carrier never sends any pre-answer audio and goes straight from
  "trying" to answered — behavior is unchanged from today.
- The caller's device itself hangs up so quickly after placing the call
  that it happens before any pre-answer audio has arrived — the call is
  abandoned the same way it is today, with no early-media state to unwind.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The system MUST relay any pre-answer audio the carrier sends
  during outbound call setup to the caller, starting as soon as that audio
  is available, rather than waiting for the call to be formally answered.
- **FR-002**: The system MUST preserve today's behavior — plain ringing,
  no audio — for outbound calls to carriers that send no pre-answer audio.
- **FR-003**: The system MUST hand off from pre-answer audio to the
  answered call's audio with zero gap — the same audio path that carried
  pre-answer audio MUST continue carrying the answered call's audio
  unchanged, with no perceptible break, reset, or re-establishment at the
  moment of answer.
- **FR-004**: The system MUST end both the local and carrier legs of a
  call cleanly when the caller abandons a call that is currently playing
  pre-answer audio.
- **FR-005**: The system MUST end the local leg cleanly when the carrier
  fails or rejects a call that had already started sending pre-answer
  audio, rather than leaving the caller connected with no further audio.
- **FR-006**: This feature MUST NOT reduce the reliability of outbound
  call setup — if relaying pre-answer audio cannot be set up for a given
  call, that call MUST still proceed with today's plain-ringing behavior
  rather than fail outright because of this feature.
- **FR-007**: The system MUST NOT relay pre-answer audio for inbound calls
  (calls arriving from the carrier) — this feature covers outbound-placed
  calls only.
- **FR-008**: The system MUST relay pre-answer audio regardless of whether
  the carrier explicitly labels it as such — any playable audio the
  carrier sends before answering qualifies, not only audio carrying a
  specific carrier-set label.
- **FR-009**: The system MUST keep the call two-way audio throughout
  pre-answer audio, the same as it does once the call is answered — it
  MUST NOT suppress the caller's own audio from reaching the carrier even
  when the carrier's pre-answer response indicates the audio is
  one-directional (e.g. an announcement).

### Key Entities

- **Outbound call attempt**: A call placed from a local extension/softphone
  through the bridge toward a carrier destination. Passes through
  "attempting," "pre-answer audio" (new, optional), and "answered" states
  before reaching "ended."

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: When a carrier sends pre-answer audio during an outbound
  call, the caller hears it starting within 1 second of the carrier
  sending it.
- **SC-002**: Outbound calls to carriers that never send pre-answer audio
  show no measurable change in call setup time or behavior compared to
  today.
- **SC-003**: When a caller abandons a call during pre-answer audio, both
  legs of the call are confirmed ended within 5 seconds — matching today's
  existing call-abandonment behavior — with no call left active or
  orphaned in call history.
- **SC-004**: The originally diagnosed scenario — a carrier announcement on
  an outbound attempt — is verifiable by placing one live test call and
  listening for the announcement, with no log or packet-capture analysis
  required.
- **SC-005**: When a call transitions from pre-answer audio to answered,
  a listener on a live test call cannot detect any break, reset, or
  restart in the audio at the moment of answer.

## Assumptions

- "The caller" is the person or system that placed the outbound call
  through the bridge's existing local extension/softphone entry point;
  this feature changes what they hear, not how calls are placed.
- Pre-answer audio is treated as available the moment the carrier sends
  any provisional response carrying it, whether or not the carrier
  explicitly labels it with a "this is early media" marker — matching
  standard call-handling expectations rather than depending on any one
  carrier's specific signaling choices (FR-008).
- If setting up the relay for a given call attempt is not possible for
  some reason, the call still proceeds — this feature is additive to
  outbound calling, never a new way for an outbound call to fail
  (FR-006).
- Reachability of a specific carrier's pre-answer announcement for manual
  verification (Story 3) is opportunistic — verification uses whichever
  carrier reproduces the behavior at test time, not a guaranteed fixture.
